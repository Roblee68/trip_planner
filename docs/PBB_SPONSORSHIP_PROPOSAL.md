# PB&B sponsored events — draft commercial design

Status: proposal, not a live ad product or published rate card. Prices below are proposed CAD prices for one event in one eligible event city; applicable sales tax is additional. There is no nonprofit discount or negotiated tier price. The organizer website has a separate free event-submission path.

## Where it lives

Use a public advertiser web page to explain rates and collect applications. The app displays approved sponsored event cards, each plainly labelled **Sponsored**. The web form does not publish an event or collect payment automatically. A private operator review view approves the organizer, event, creative, city, and campaign dates. Ordinary users cannot submit a paid event directly inside PB&B Social or the event catalogue.

A sponsorship is a placement for an existing, source-linked event. It does not change editorial rankings or imply that PB&B organizes the event, guarantees a ticket, or guarantees the organizer's conduct. Every card links to the organizer's official HTTPS event page. Do not sell promotional push alerts or access to Social chats.

## Earlier fixed-tier sketch — subject to inventory review

Reserve a conservative **30% platform-fee allowance** in paid sponsorship pricing even if the final sales route has no Apple commission. This is a pricing buffer, not a statement that Apple must or will be paid for a web sale. The amounts below round up the earlier $49/$129/$249 net targets after dividing by 0.70. Keep the listed amounts as proposed gross prices and revisit them after PB&B has measured monthly active users, city-level ad views, sponsor clicks, and repeat bookings. A registered-user count alone is not evidence that a higher price will deliver value. No purchase flow has been implemented.

| Tier | Cost | Run | One-city placement | Example position |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| Local | CAD $79 + applicable tax | 7 consecutive days | One Sponsored card in This Week | A compact event row after organic results for that day |
| Featured | CAD $189 + applicable tax | 14 consecutive days | Local placement plus one Sponsored card in Discover | A full event card after the first organic results |
| Spotlight | CAD $359 + applicable tax | 30 consecutive days | Featured placements with priority for the first rotating Sponsored position in Discover | A larger Discover card with image, still below the leading organic content |

One fee covers one event, one selected city, and one run. All placements are identified as Sponsored on the card itself. A Spotlight booking has priority when the rotating sponsored slot is selected, subject to its guaranteed-view pacing. Featured and Local inventory must also be capped so organic results remain the main experience. The guaranteed viewable-impression count is quoted only after city inventory is measured; there is no attendance guarantee. Show the event only to people browsing its event city or who chose that city as a place they would travel to. A campaign ends at its purchased run end **or 30 minutes before the event starts, whichever is earlier**. Only offer a tier when the event is far enough away to deliver its full run; schedule the run to end at the cutoff. If PB&B cannot deliver the full approved run, do not start or charge the campaign; if an active run is interrupted by PB&B, credit or refund the undelivered portion.

The advertiser page must display the exact price, duration, city scope, placement, tax treatment, the quoted view guarantee, and no-attendance-guarantee statement before application. Do not silently renew. An organizer can request a new campaign for another date after review.

## Rotation and guaranteed views

A paid event is **not shown on every visit**. Keep one sponsored position per eligible feed view, after organic content; leave some visits with no sponsored card. Rotate eligible, approved campaigns for the event city according to remaining contracted views and days left. Give a behind-schedule campaign more opportunities without ignoring per-person frequency caps. A person should not see the same campaign more than once per day or see sponsorship on every feed visit. Do not show a campaign after its event cutoff or to a city outside its approved audience.

Contract for **X viewable card impressions** in a named city and run window, with X displayed on the order before payment. A view counts only when at least 50% of the card is onscreen for one continuous second while the app is foregrounded; loading a card or scrolling past it does not count. A repeated look in the same session does not count twice. Views are **not unique people**; report both counted views and distinct reach. Deduplicate client events on the server and retain only the minimal pseudonymous data needed for caps, billing, and fraud checks. The 50%/one-second threshold follows the IAB/MRC display-ad convention: https://www.iab.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/IAB_Retail_Media_Measurement_Guidelines_January2024.pdf

Do not assign a universal X to these tiers until PB&B has at least four weeks of actual viewable inventory by city and placement. For each proposed campaign, forecast eligible views during its dates, subtract already promised views, apply the rotation and frequency caps, and reserve no more than half of the remaining conservative forecast. Show the exact guaranteed X before checkout; if the city cannot support it, offer a smaller contract or decline the booking. Record the booked guarantee, delivered count, pause history, and source-link taps. If PB&B misses X, extend only if the event has not reached its cutoff; otherwise refund the undelivered share or provide an advertiser-approved replacement campaign. Google Ad Manager uses analogous pacing and per-user frequency caps for guaranteed campaigns: https://support.google.com/admanager/answer/2669484 and https://support.google.com/admanager/answer/82242

At only 1,000 monthly active users spread among four cities, a promise of thousands of views **per local sponsor** would likely require excessive repeat exposure or more distribution channels. Do not publish such a guarantee based only on registered-user count. A higher-value campaign can combine separately measured app, website, and opt-in email inventory once those channels have real reach; never combine their counts without disclosing the breakdown.

## Free organizer intake and unsold inventory

The organizer website offers **free editorial consideration** before any paid campaign exists. Every submitter creates an account with a verified business-domain email and supplies legal/trading business names, business address and phone, representative name, official business website, public event URL, event date/time, venue, city, price, description, and optional registration number. Up to three organizer-provided HTTPS image links may accompany a versioned, explicit, event-scoped permission to display/crop those images in PB&B's app and website. No general permission is inferred from a website link. PB&B reviews each source and image before use. The form stores a sponsorship preference separately from optional consent for later promotional email. A free submission does not guarantee publication, ranking, views, or a reply by a specified date. No event is automatically published or sent to Social.

When no sponsor has booked a header, use the normal PB&B image and tagline. When a Discover Weekly Showcase slot is unsold, use a PB&B editorial event pick. Never mark fallback inventory Sponsored, and never promise an unsold slot to a future advertiser until its booked dates and review are confirmed.

## Organizer and event review

1. Require a registered organizer account and a **business-domain email address**. Send a one-time link to verify control of that mailbox. Reject free consumer mail domains for sponsorship. The email domain must match the organizer's HTTPS website or be demonstrably linked from its official site; a mismatched domain requires a human explanation and independent proof.
2. Require the legal/trading business name, website, public contact details, event title, date, start time, venue address, ticket price, official event URL, and the name of the person authorized to buy the placement. Keep the business email private from app users.
3. Independently open the event page and verify the title, date, time, venue, organizer, ticket price, and booking destination. Check the venue or official organizer site if there is uncertainty. Do not accept a social post, image, or screenshot as the only event source. Reject ticket resale, personal payment requests, cryptocurrency, prepaid card requests, or off-platform chat as a booking method.
4. Confirm that the applicant controls the event or has written authorization to promote it. Record the reviewer, evidence URLs, decision, and review date. Recheck the source before the campaign starts and periodically while it runs; pause if the page disappears, the event is cancelled, details change materially, or fraud is reported.
5. Keep submission and payment separate. Payment approval never bypasses editorial/fraud review. Rejected events receive no placement. Give users a visible **Report this event** action and a route to support; an operator needs a removal and refund process.

Website and email checks reduce risk but cannot certify an event as safe. Avoid a consumer-facing “verified event” badge; use **Sponsored** and a source link. The same source checks should apply to any event that members bring into Social before it is promoted or widely notified.

## Data and disclosure

Target by the selected event city only, not exact GPS, chat content, gender, or sensitive interests. On the ad detail show “Why am I seeing this? You selected [city]” (or “You are browsing [city]”). Advertisers get aggregate impressions and source-link taps, with no member identities or Social profiles. Record clicks without handing the advertiser personal identifiers. Label every paid placement prominently. App users can report an ad and organic events remain visible.

### Premium sponsored image headers

An approved advertiser may replace the entire illustration and **all PB&B slogan text within the tab header** with its own finished image. “Make a story of it,” “Find the unexpected,” and other PB&B hero copy do not overlay paid artwork. The tab navigation and app chrome still identify the screen. Render an unmistakable **Sponsored** label and an official-site action in app UI outside the creative, so neither obscures the advertiser's logo. The normal PB&B header returns when no eligible campaign is selected. The advertiser supplies and owns or licenses every image, logo, and line of text; PB&B reviews the final crop at phone and tablet sizes before approval.

**Draft creative specification for the current 156-point-high header:** submit two static sRGB files: a mobile image **2400 × 1000 px** (2.4:1) and a wide/tablet image **3200 × 800 px** (4:1), PNG or high-quality JPEG, at most **2 MB each**. No SVG, GIF, video, or transparent background. Keep essential logo, event name, and any text inside the central **70% of width and 60% of height**; the outer area may be cropped. Use large, high-contrast text: a short sponsor name and one short headline, with no small-print event facts in the image. PB&B supplies the app-rendered sponsor disclosure and official HTTPS link beneath it. Preview the uploaded files in the actual device frames; reject a crop if any essential text is cut or unreadable. A later responsive layout change requires updating these dimensions and re-approving active artwork.

Allowed: a rights-cleared photo or illustration; the organizer's real name/logo; truthful event or venue imagery and concise offer text. Disallowed: PB&B branding or a claim that PB&B endorses or organizes the event; fake app controls, unreadable legal terms, QR codes, phone numbers, social handles, direct-payment instructions, countdown/false scarcity claims, explicit or discriminatory content, and ticket prices or dates that disagree with the official page. Do not place important details in the image alone; the linked event record carries date, place, price, and accessibility details. Require organizer website and business-email verification plus human approval of the image, its text, destination, city, and event status. Pause the header if the event changes or the image no longer matches its landing page.

A Discover header can promote a local venue, attraction, tourism partner, or upcoming approved event. A This Week header must lead to an event in the selected city and week. A Now header needs a currently reachable event that starts more than 30 minutes away. An Along the Way header must genuinely fit the chosen route and detour; since the current header appears before route entry, show PB&B's normal image until a route has been submitted, or use a sponsored card within the route results instead. Do not sell headers on Saved, Settings, or private Social screens in the first launch. Show at most one sponsored header per feed visit and rotate by campaign view obligation and per-person cap. Give the header its own view guarantee and report, separate from any card views; do not count a header and a card as one view or imply they reached two people.

This should sit **above** Spotlight in the eventual rate card, but no premium price or fixed view promise should be published until four weeks of header view data exist by tab and city. Reserve the highest-value inventory for a small number of campaigns. The price must reflect actual unique reach and source-link taps.

### Discover Weekly Showcase — three large cards

Discover already has three large image-led **PB&B Picks** at its bottom. They are editorial today, with no paid organizer placement. Keep that visual format and make up to **three separately bookable weekly showcase slots** for approved real events. A paid card gets its own **Sponsored** label and official-source action; an unsold slot can show an editorial **PB&B Pick**. If paid and editorial cards mix, use a neutral section heading and never show the blanket “No organizer paid” claim. Each sponsored card stays in its assigned position for **seven consecutive days**, rather than rotating between advertisers during that booking. The event must remain upcoming and source-verified for the full booking; sell a seven-day run only if it can end at least 30 minutes before the event starts. If an event cancels or the source changes, remove it immediately and refund or credit the undelivered days. Report actual viewable card impressions and official-site taps, but sell the fixed week and position rather than promising unlimited views.

The Weekly Showcase is distinct from rotating sponsored cards elsewhere in Discover. Its fixed presence is more valuable, so quote it as a separate premium seven-day product after city-level inventory is measured. Its advertiser page should show a phone preview, exact city, slot position, seven dates, price, and expected reach before purchase. No campaign or price is live yet.

### PB&B placement menu to test

| Placement | Suitable sponsor | User rule | First pricing model |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

| **This Week event card** | Approved event organizer | Match chosen event city and date; label Sponsored; keep organic events first | Fixed 7/14-day campaign with a measured, quoted view guarantee |
| **Now event card** | Approved same-day event | Only if start is more than 30 minutes away, venue/source are current, and travel time makes it feasible | Add-on to an event campaign; quote separately after traffic is measured |
| **Along the Way stop** | Verified venue, attraction, or timed event | Match the actual planned route and allowed detour; no fake proximity | Pilot manually, then consider capped cost per source/directions click |
| **Discover Weekly Showcase** | Organizer of a real, source-verified upcoming event | Up to three large cards at Discover bottom, fixed for seven days; per-card Sponsored label; editorial fallback for unsold slots | Separate seven-day premium booking, quoted after view data exists |
| **Discover sponsored event card** | Approved event organizer | After organic picks; relevant to chosen city and interests without sensitive targeting | Premium add-on to This Week; rotated and capped |
| **Website or opt-in weekly email** | Approved event or local partner | Show separate channel counts and get marketing consent where required | Future bundle only after these channels have measurable reach |

Do not put sponsorship inside private Social chats or imply that a sponsor is a Social member, event host, or safety endorser. Count a person who sees the same creative on Week and Now as two views but one reached person; report both. Keep source-link and directions taps as separate outcomes.

**Initial commercial recommendation:** start with two products rather than a crowded rate card: (1) a sponsored event campaign in This Week, eligible for contextual Now/Discover exposure, and (2) a seven-day Discover Weekly Showcase slot. Reserve Along the Way for a closely monitored pilot. Offer a city-specific quoted view minimum only after telemetry supports it. Expand to multi-surface bundles as audience and sales evidence grow.

**Recommendation:** do not build a separate organizer app for the first launch. Build a responsive advertiser web application and a private web review queue. Add campaign status, invoices, and aggregate results to that web portal only after repeated organizer demand. Keep the attendee app focused on discovery and Social. A dedicated organizer app would add separate iOS/Android releases, store reviews, authentication, support, and maintenance without improving the first fraud review workflow.

## Payment and launch decision

Apple explicitly describes **free apps with display advertising** as a model in which the developer earns ad revenue. Apple describes its commission on sales of paid apps and in-app purchases, not on ordinary third-party display-ad receipts. A game showing another game's AdMob ad is the standard example: the ad network pays the publisher. That is distinct from a player or organizer buying a digital boost inside the same app. Sources: https://developer.apple.com/app-store/business-models/ and https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/getting-paid/view-payments-and-proceeds/ and https://developers.google.com/admob/ios/quick-start

For PB&B, paid event placements sold to businesses through an independent advertiser website resemble publisher advertising inventory more than an in-app user boost. **This is an inference from the published models, not an express Apple approval of our proposed sales flow.** The attendee app should show the ad and its source, with no advertiser purchase or purchase link there.

A separate advertiser web page is useful for rates, examples, and applications. **Do not add a Stripe checkout or send payment links yet.** Apple's App Review Guideline 3.1.3(g) makes three distinct points: (1) buying an ad or boost **inside the PB&B attendee app** to display in that same app requires in-app purchase; (2) a **separate app** devoted solely to managing campaigns across media types, which displays no ads itself, has an advertising-management exception; and (3) the quoted clause does **not expressly decide** whether an organizer can buy a PB&B placement entirely on an external website. A PB&B-only organizer app should not be assumed to qualify for an exception written for campaigns across media types. Do not add a purchase link or checkout inside the attendee app. Obtain platform-specific advice or App Review clarification for the proposed web-only sales flow before taking payment; also check Google Play billing rules, its ad declaration, and tax setup. The web portal remains the simpler operational choice even if a separate management app could eventually qualify.

Before public launch: approve the rates, build the reviewed submission flow and operator queue, implement capped city-based placements with sponsorship labels and reporting, establish the approved payment flow, update the privacy notice and store ad disclosures, and test campaign start/stop and event cancellation.
